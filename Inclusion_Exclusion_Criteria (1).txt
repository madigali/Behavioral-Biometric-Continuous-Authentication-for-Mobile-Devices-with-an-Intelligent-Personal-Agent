Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria
--------------------------------

Inclusion Criteria:
1. Peer-reviewed journal articles, conference proceedings, or
   systematic literature reviews.
2. Published between 2017 and 2025.
3. Written in English.
4. Focus on continuous mobile user authentication, behavioral
   biometrics, or multimodal biometrics on smartphones/wearables.
5. Studies employing built-in smartphone or wearable sensors
   (accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, touchscreen) explicitly
   for user verification, identification, or authentication.
6. Empirical studies, experimental evaluations, systematic reviews,
   or well-founded theoretical/architectural papers.
7. Studies involving machine learning, deep learning, or AI-based
   classification methods for biometric authentication.
8. Papers addressing intelligent personal assistants (IPA),
   privacy-preserving authentication, or federated learning in the
   context of mobile security.

Exclusion Criteria:
1. Conference abstracts, posters, editorials, book reviews, or
   opinion pieces without empirical content.
2. Studies not related to mobile authentication, behavioral
   biometrics, or user security on mobile/wearable devices.
3. Papers focusing solely on physiological biometrics
   (fingerprint, iris, face) without behavioral or continuous
   authentication components.
4. Studies that do not implement a user verification or
   authentication task on mobile or wearable platforms.
5. Non-English publications.
6. Duplicate records identified across databases.
7. Papers without accessible full text.
8. Studies with insufficient methodological rigor or lacking
   clear performance metrics (e.g., FAR, FRR, EER, accuracy).

Application of Criteria:
Inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied in two stages:
title/abstract screening and full-text review. Criteria were
operationalized using a standardized checklist aligned with
PRISMA 2020 guidelines. Ambiguous cases were resolved through
iterative reassessment against the predefined research questions.
All data were used solely for the purposes of this systematic
review, in accordance with ethical research standards.
